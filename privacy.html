Privacy

What happens to what you send me

This site is a personal one. It has no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. The only information I receive is what you choose to write in the contact form.

Who is responsible

Elina Teplygina, Zürich, Switzerland. For anything on this page, including a request to see or delete your data, write to REPLACE_PRIVACY_EMAIL.

The contact form

The form asks for your name, your email address and a description of your situation. You decide what to put in it, and nothing on this site requires you to fill it in.

I use what you send to reply to you and, if it fits, to arrange a conversation. I do not use it for anything else, and I do not sell it, share it or add you to a mailing list.

The form itself is built and hosted by Tally, which means your answers are stored in my account with them as well as reaching my inbox. Tally publishes its own privacy policy on its website.

Hosting

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web server, GitHub's records each request, which includes the visitor's IP address, for the purpose of serving the site and keeping it secure. I do not have access to those logs.

Fonts

The typefaces are loaded from Google Fonts when the page opens, which means your browser contacts Google's servers and your IP address reaches them as part of that request. No cookie is set by this.

Cookies

This site sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser. There is nothing here to consent to, which is why you are not being asked to.

The contact form is embedded from tally.so and runs in its own frame, so anything it stores is governed by Tally's policy rather than mine.

How long I keep things

I keep messages for as long as the conversation they belong to is still useful, and I delete them when it is over or whenever you ask me to, whichever comes first.

Your rights

You can ask me what I hold about you, ask me to correct it, and ask me to delete it. Write to the address above and I will do it. You do not need to give a reason, and I will not ask for one.

If you are in the EU or the EEA, the GDPR gives you these rights; in Switzerland, the Federal Act on Data Protection does. In practice my answer is the same either way.
